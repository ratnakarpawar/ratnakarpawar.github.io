Supervised Program for Alignment Research · mentored by Rishub Jain (Google DeepMind)

  • Researching human-AI complementarity for harm identification: how to combine human expertise with model capabilities to improve safety evaluation coverage and reliability, building on DeepMind's collaborative safety work
  • Built and released a source-grounded benchmark for when rules should and shouldn't treat people differently: 817 paired items from 43 institutional sources across 10 jurisdictions. Generated cross-vendor so no model audits its own output, after finding same-vendor generation inflates scores by up to 15 points
  • Found that models track current norms rather than the rule actually cited. On pairs where two versions of a guideline differ only in whether a demographic axis is a legitimate input, a seven-model panel drops to 0.22 on the superseded rule
  • Measured the human side with a rating study: raters showed none of the models' systematic asymmetry on equal-treatment items, and were weakest exactly where models are strong. Evidence that human and model errors are differently distributed, which is the precondition for oversight to add anything
  • Surveyed multimodal agentic safety benchmarks (computer-use, browser, and tool-use trajectories) for oversight headroom, finding most already saturated for frontier models as trajectory monitors, then built the pipeline to construct data that isn't: extract verified criteria from authoritative sources, perturb them into paired items with known ground truth

A benchmark for when a rule should treat people differently, and when it shouldn't. 817 paired items built from verbatim institutional sources across 10 jurisdictions, including the hard cases where a demographic axis is a legitimate input. The sharpest finding comes from rules that changed their minds: cite a superseded guideline and models apply today's version instead, down to 0.22.
